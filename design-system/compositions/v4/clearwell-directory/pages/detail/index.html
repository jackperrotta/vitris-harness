Service / The monthly rhythm

A handover
you can understand.

Responsibility is useful when it is visible. These three parts describe what to discuss before agreeing a bookkeeping scope.

01 Reconcile02 Report03 Hand over
01 / Reconciliation

Set a rhythm for the records.

Begin with a sample of the records your studio already keeps. The first question is practical: what arrives, who supplies it and when can it be handed over?

The monthly reconciliation scope should name those responsibilities. It gives the routine a place in the month rather than leaving it as an open-ended task.

What should I bring to the conversation?

A representative sample of your records and a description of how you currently collect them. Avoid sending sensitive financial information through an ordinary inquiry email.

Ask about reconciliation ↗
02 / Monthly reporting

Make the month readable.

A report needs an agreed purpose. Talk through what you need to understand and how you prefer to review it. The service scope can then connect the records to a useful reporting rhythm.

Reporting does not supply investment advice. It makes the agreed information available for your own decisions and for the specialists you choose to consult.

Discuss the reporting questions ↗
03 / Year-end handover

Leave a clear next step.

Agree the recipient, the records they require and the way the transfer should be organised. That boundary matters: preparing a handover is different from preparing or filing a tax return.

Tax returns and investment advice remain outside this fictional service. The final agreement should make the responsibilities clear to everyone involved.

Before work starts.

  1. Bring a sample of your records.
  2. Agree the scope together.
  3. Set a monthly handover.
